# Three-Phase AWS Certification Exam Strategy

## Overview

This lesson presents a time-management method for completing a timed AWS certification exam. The method classifies questions by the effort and confidence they require, answers high-confidence questions first, reserves most time for solvable medium-difficulty questions, and postpones unusually difficult questions until the end.

## Exam Alignment

- **Primary SCS-C03 Domain:** Security Foundations and Governance
- **Secondary Domain(s):** None
- **Main AWS Services:** None; this is an exam-strategy lesson
- **Lesson Type:** Text-Based Reference
- **Exam Importance:** Supporting
- **Core Skill:** Allocate exam time so difficult questions do not prevent completion of higher-confidence questions.

## The Three Phases

1. **Phase 1 — high-confidence questions:** Move through the entire exam and answer questions whose solution is immediately clear. Mark uncertain questions for review instead of spending disproportionate time on them.
2. **Phase 2 — deliberate analysis:** Revisit the remaining questions, skip those that are still unusually difficult, and spend the largest share of time on questions that can be solved through careful comparison and elimination.
3. **Phase 3 — difficult questions:** Use the remaining time on the hardest marked questions. Ensure every question has an answer before time expires because unanswered questions cannot earn credit.

The easy/medium/hard proportions in the source are personal estimates, not properties guaranteed by AWS. Difficulty is subjective and depends on the candidate's strengths.

## Practical Workflow

- Track remaining time and question count throughout the exam.
- Use **Mark for Review** whenever confidence is low or another question might later trigger recall.
- Avoid changing a high-confidence answer without a specific technical reason.
- Read multi-response instructions carefully and select exactly the requested number.
- Practice the method under timed conditions; the workflow should be familiar before exam day.
- Use missed practice questions to identify knowledge gaps, then return to authoritative documentation and retest.

## Common Exam Traps

- Spending several minutes on an early hard question creates time pressure on easier questions later.
- Treating a subjective difficulty split as a guaranteed exam distribution is unsafe.
- Selecting an answer because it sounds familiar is weaker than matching every stated requirement.
- Leaving a question unanswered provides no benefit; make the best supported selection before time expires.

## Memory Hooks

### One-Line Memory Hook

> **Secure the quick points, earn the reasoning points, then spend the remainder on the hardest points.**

### Mnemonic

> **E-M-H: Easy first, Medium most, Hard last.**

## Quick Review

- Complete a fast first pass and answer only high-confidence questions.
- Spend most of the exam on questions that reward deliberate analysis.
- Mark difficult questions and defer them rather than breaking your pace.
- Monitor time continuously.
- Answer every question before submission.
- Treat practice scores as diagnostics, not as guarantees of an exam result.

## Self-Check Questions

1. Why should a difficult early question be deferred?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

It can consume time needed for multiple questions that the candidate is more likely to answer correctly.

</details>

2. Which phase should normally receive the most time?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Phase 2, which contains questions that require thought but remain reasonably solvable.

</details>

3. What is the purpose of marking a question for review?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

It preserves momentum while providing a clear queue of uncertain questions to revisit later.

</details>
