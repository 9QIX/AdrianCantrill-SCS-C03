# AWS Service Catalog for Governed Provisioning

## Overview

AWS Service Catalog lets organizations publish approved infrastructure products that users can provision under controlled constraints. It reduces configuration variance while separating portfolio administration from product consumption.

## Exam Alignment

- **Primary SCS-C03 Domain:** Security Foundations and Governance
- **Secondary Domain(s):** Infrastructure Security
- **Main AWS Services:** AWS Service Catalog, AWS CloudFormation, IAM
- **Lesson Type:** Architecture
- **Exam Importance:** Medium
- **Core Skill:** Use approved products, portfolios, constraints, and launch roles for governed self-service.

## Core Concepts

- A **product** is a deployable approved resource definition, commonly backed by CloudFormation.
- A **portfolio** groups products with access controls.
- **Constraints** limit how products launch, update, or vary.
- A **launch role** lets Service Catalog provision through controlled permissions instead of giving end users direct resource-creation authority.
- Product versions allow controlled evolution and retirement.

## Policy or Permission Analysis

Users need permission to access the portfolio and provision its product. With a launch constraint, the service assumes the designated role to create resources. The role's permissions are therefore a critical maximum for product actions; `iam:PassRole` and trust relationships must be scoped carefully.

## Exam Decision Guide

| Requirement | Choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approved self-service infrastructure | AWS Service Catalog | Curates products and constrains provisioning |
| General infrastructure-as-code deployment | AWS CloudFormation | Creates stacks but does not itself provide the same catalog/portfolio experience |
| Organization-wide permission ceiling | SCP | Restricts actions regardless of catalog use |

## Common Exam Traps

- Service Catalog does not replace CloudFormation; it can govern CloudFormation-backed products.
- Product approval does not guarantee runtime compliance if users can modify resources outside the catalog.
- Overly broad launch roles undermine separation of duties.

## Memory Hooks

> **CloudFormation builds; Service Catalog curates who may launch approved builds.**

## Quick Review

- Products are grouped into portfolios.
- Access and constraints govern consumption.
- Launch roles separate user access from provisioning authority.
- Version products deliberately.
- Combine catalogs with guardrails and configuration monitoring.

## Self-Check Questions

1. Why use a launch role?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It lets users provision an approved product through controlled service permissions without granting them all underlying create permissions directly.

</details>

2. What is the authorization role of a Service Catalog portfolio?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A portfolio groups approved products and controls user access to them. Launch constraints can make products run under a designated role, separating end-user access from provisioning permission.

</details>

3. Why can an allowed user still fail to launch a product?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The product artifact, launch role, constraint, template, quota, or underlying service permissions can fail even when portfolio access is correct. Inspect the provisioned-product events.

</details>
