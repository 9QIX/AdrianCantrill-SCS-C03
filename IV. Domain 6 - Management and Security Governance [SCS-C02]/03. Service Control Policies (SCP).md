# Service Control Policies

## Overview

Service control policies (SCPs) are AWS Organizations guardrails that define the maximum permissions available to principals in affected member accounts. They do not grant permissions and do not affect the management account.

## Exam Alignment

- **Primary SCS-C03 Domain:** Security Foundations and Governance
- **Secondary Domain(s):** Identity and Access Management
- **Main AWS Services:** AWS Organizations, IAM
- **Lesson Type:** Policy Analysis
- **Exam Importance:** High
- **Core Skill:** Apply and troubleshoot organization-wide permission guardrails without confusing them with grants.

## Core Concepts

- SCPs require an organization with all features enabled.
- An SCP may attach to the organization root, an organizational unit (OU), or an account; inherited policies combine down the hierarchy.
- Effective permissions are limited by every applicable SCP level and by normal IAM evaluation.
- The account root user in a member account is constrained by SCPs; the organization management account is not.
- SCPs do not affect service-linked roles.

## Policy or Permission Analysis

For an action to succeed, an applicable identity or resource policy must allow it, the SCP path must permit it, and no applicable explicit deny may exist. Permission boundaries and session policies may restrict it further.

Two common models are:

- **Deny list:** keep `FullAWSAccess` and add targeted denies.
- **Allow list:** remove broad access and explicitly permit only approved services/actions at every relevant hierarchy level.

An SCP has no `Principal` element because attachment scope supplies the affected accounts. Conditions can exempt carefully controlled role ARNs, restrict Regions, or require resource attributes, but unsupported global actions and service behavior must be considered.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Verification | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Administrator receives `AccessDenied` | SCP deny or missing allow in an allow-list model | Inspect policies inherited from root, OU, and account; review CloudTrail | Adjust the narrowest appropriate guardrail |
| New service unusable in an OU | Allow-list SCP does not include it | Compare effective SCP path with requested action | Add reviewed service actions at every required level |
| Management-account action still succeeds | SCPs do not constrain management account | Confirm caller account | Minimize management-account use and apply other controls |

## Common Exam Traps

- SCPs limit; they never grant.
- Moving an account changes inherited guardrails.
- `FullAWSAccess` does not override a separate explicit deny.
- An SCP cannot constrain the management account.
- Resource policies do not generally bypass an applicable SCP ceiling.

## Memory Hooks

> **SCP sets the ceiling; IAM provides the room beneath it; explicit deny closes the door.**

## Quick Review

- SCPs apply to member accounts through hierarchy.
- They do not grant permissions.
- Explicit deny overrides allow.
- Allow-list designs require permission at each applicable level.
- Test changes in a controlled OU.
- Preserve protected administration and recovery paths.

## Self-Check Questions

1. Can an SCP grant Amazon S3 read access?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

No. An identity or resource policy must grant access; the SCP only limits the maximum.

</details>

2. Why can an administrator policy fail in a member account?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

An SCP, permission boundary, session policy, resource policy, or other applicable explicit deny can restrict the request.

</details>

3. Does an SCP grant an IAM principal permission?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. An SCP defines the maximum permissions available in affected member accounts. An identity- or resource-based allow is still required, and an applicable explicit deny wins.

</details>
