# Demonstration: Applying Service Control Policies

## Overview

This demonstration uses AWS Organizations to attach SCPs and observe their effect in member accounts. It shows that guardrails restrict effective permissions even when a member-account identity has an administrator policy.

## Exam Alignment

- **Primary SCS-C03 Domain:** Security Foundations and Governance
- **Secondary Domain(s):** Identity and Access Management
- **Main AWS Services:** AWS Organizations, IAM, AWS CloudTrail
- **Lesson Type:** Demonstration / Policy Analysis
- **Exam Importance:** High
- **Core Skill:** Safely attach, test, and troubleshoot an SCP across an organization hierarchy.

## Demonstration Goal

Apply a targeted organizational restriction, confirm it in a member account, and restore access without losing administrative control.

## Prerequisites

- An AWS Organization with all features and SCP policy type enabled.
- Management-account permission to create and attach SCPs.
- A test member account and non-production OU.
- Member-account test identity and CloudTrail visibility.

## Demonstration Steps

1. Record the organization root, OU, account, and currently attached SCPs.
2. Create a narrowly scoped SCP using either a targeted explicit deny or an intentional allow-list design.
3. Validate policy syntax and preserve an authorized recovery path.
4. Attach the SCP first to the test OU or account, not an untested production root.
5. In the member account, test one action expected to succeed and one expected to fail.
6. Inspect the denied request and its CloudTrail event; correlate the action, Region, resource, and principal with the guardrail.
7. Detach or revise the test SCP and verify restored behavior.

## Validation

- The prohibited action fails in the affected member account despite a broad identity-policy allow.
- Unrelated permitted actions still work.
- The management account is not constrained by the SCP.
- Moving or detaching the account changes inherited behavior as expected.

## Cleanup

Detach the test SCP from the test organizational unit or account, restore the intended policy attachments, and verify access from the member account. Delete the test SCP only after confirming that it is no longer attached and is not required for another exercise.

## What the Demonstration Proves

Effective authorization is an intersection of grants and guardrails. SCPs centrally prevent member accounts from granting or exercising permissions outside the approved maximum.

## Common Exam Traps

- An `AccessDenied` response does not identify which policy layer denied the request.
- Removing `FullAWSAccess` before a complete allow-list is ready can cause broad outages.
- A policy attached to an OU also affects nested OUs and accounts.
- Testing only with the management account cannot prove an SCP works.

## Memory Hooks

> **Attach low, test both allow and deny, then expand carefully.**

## Quick Review

- Use a non-production OU for initial tests.
- Validate positive and negative cases.
- Check inherited policies at every hierarchy level.
- SCPs affect member accounts, including their root users.
- CloudTrail supplies request evidence but policy inspection identifies the guardrail.

## Self-Check Questions

1. Why test an SCP with a member-account administrator?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It demonstrates that the organizational guardrail limits even broadly allowed member-account identities.

</details>

2. Why must the SCP test use a member account?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

SCPs do not restrict principals in the organization management account, so a management-account test cannot prove the guardrail works.

</details>

3. How should the lab guardrail be cleaned up?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Detach the test SCP from the target organizational unit or account, restore the intended production policy attachments, and verify effective access from a member account before deleting the unused test policy.

</details>
